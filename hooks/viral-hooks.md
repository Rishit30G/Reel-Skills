# 100 Viral Hooks: swipe file

Source: *The Brandit Content Swipe File, 100 Viral Hooks* (`hooks/100-Viral-Hooks.pdf`). Views as collected in October 2026. The hooks are numbered by views, biggest first.

## How to use this when writing a hook

1. Start from what the video says, then pick the hook types that fit it best (see the index).
2. Fill in the `(insert …)` blanks with the video's own industry, numbers and words. Leave the rest of the sentence alone: the rhythm is the point.
3. Check the original reel (the **Watch** link). What is said and shown in the first 3 seconds matters as much as the words.
4. Offer **three** filled-in versions so they can be tested as trial reels. Keep the winner.
5. In Motion Studio the hook must land in the first 2 seconds of the film (see the contact-sheet score in CLAUDE.md).
6. Before you hand them over, check the hooks, the script and the CTA against the [line swaps](#line-swaps-what-to-say-instead) (S1-S6). Replace any weak line.

## Line swaps: what to say instead

Source: viewcci reel, "Here's why the words you use decide whether people stay"
([Watch](https://www.instagram.com/viewcci/reel/DenWEoGOy0O/), 28 s, posted October 17, 2026; 269 likes when watched on 2026-11-06).
It's six "Don't say / Instead say" pairs. The weak line is a habit that gives
people a reason to leave. The strong line sounds sure of itself and gives them a reason to stay.
S1 and S2 are openers, so they count as hooks: fill in the blanks like any other hook.
S3-S6 are lines from the middle and end of a video. Use them on the script and the CTA.

### S1
**Don't say:** "Hey guys, welcome back."  
**Instead say:** "If this is you, then keep watching."  
**Hook:** If you (insert situation the viewer is in), then keep watching.  
**Why:** Skips the greeting. The viewer recognises themselves in the first second.  
**Type:** `swap` (opener)

### S2
**Don't say:** "Today, I'll show you how to…"  
**Instead say:** "Here is the absolute best way to…"  
**Hook:** Here is the absolute best way to (insert goal).  
**Why:** Promises the best answer, not just a tutorial.  
**Type:** `swap` (opener)

### S3
**Don't say:** "I'm not an expert, but…"  
**Instead say:** "This is what worked for me."  
**Why:** Talks about your own result instead of apologising for it.  
**Type:** `swap` (authority)

### S4
**Don't say:** "There's a few things you could do."  
**Instead say:** "Here's 3 things you can do right now."  
**Hook:** Here's (insert #) things you can do right now to (insert result).  
**Why:** A number and "right now" make it concrete and urgent. Vague "a few" and "could" don't.  
**Type:** `swap` (list)

### S5
**Don't say:** "Wait until the end…"  
**Instead say:** "And you need to follow the last step."  
**Why:** Gives a reason to stay that's worth something, not a teaser.  
**Type:** `swap` (retention)

### S6
**Don't say:** "Make sure to drop a follow."  
**Instead say:** "Follow for part 2." (The reel's on-screen joke: "but, like, actually do.")  
**Why:** Follow for something specific coming next, not as a favour.  
**Type:** `swap` (CTA)

**How the reel shows it (format worth copying):** talking head, two stacked text cards.
A pink "Don't Say" or yellow "Instead Say" label sits above the quote in a typewriter font.
The quote fades in about 0.25 s after the label. The pairs alternate every ~2 s.
That fits the CLAUDE.md "something new every 2-4 s" rule.
